Linking / connecting ideas — reuse these
Furthermore
Adds a supporting point in the same direction
In addition to
Adds a second benefit or point (more formal than "also")
Therefore / Consequently
Shows a logical result — use after giving a reason
For example / For instance
Introduces evidence or an illustration
Because / since
Gives a reason directly in the same sentence
In conclusion
Opens the final paragraph — don't use "To conclude" and "In conclusion" together
Academic adverbs — the ones that sound professional
arguably
Signals your opinion carefully — "this is arguably the best approach"
consequently
More formal than "so" — use to show a result
preventative / preventive
Both correct — use to describe actions that stop problems before they happen
significantly / considerably
Stronger than "very much" — "considerably less costly"
largely / primarily
Softens a strong claim — "I largely agree" shows nuance
increasingly
Shows a growing trend — perfect for introductions
firmly / strongly
Shows conviction in the conclusion — "I firmly believe"
equally / similarly
Draws a parallel — "diet plays an equally critical role"
Concession / contrast — shows balance
However
Classic contrast — always at the start of the concession paragraph
Nevertheless / Nonetheless
Stronger than "however" — great in the conclusion
While / Although
Acknowledges the other side in the same sentence — use in intro
rather than
Contrasts two options — "as a solution rather than a standalone fix"
Avoid overusing "Moreover" and "Additionally" — examiners see these in every essay. "Furthermore," "In addition to," and "What is more" are fresher alternatives that show the same range.
